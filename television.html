Is your TV quietly running up your power bill?

Among Australian TVs registered in the 2026 Energy Rating data, a typical 55 inch TV with a 1 star rating uses about 755 kWh a year. A model of the same size with 6 stars or more uses only about 255 kWh. That is nearly 3 times the energy, and roughly $143 more in electricity every year.

Why this matters

Every television sold in Australia carries an Energy Rating Label. The label shows a star rating and an estimated number of kilowatt hours (kWh) used per year. The estimate comes from standardized testing that assumes the TV is on for 10 hours a day, so it is a way to compare models fairly, not a prediction of your own bill.

Multiply the kWh figure by your electricity tariff to estimate a yearly running cost. This page uses 28.55 cents per kWh, the average tariff quoted by the Energy Rating program. Your own tariff will differ, so check your bill.

The data covers more than 4,500 approved TV models sold in Australia. Half of the models use less than 397 kWh a year, which is about $113 a year at that tariff.

How to read the chart

Each group of bars is one star rating, and moving right means a higher rating. The height of a bar is the median electricity used per year, so moving up means more energy. Colour shows the screen size category: small (43" or less), medium (44" to 65") and large (66" or more). Hover over a bar to see its exact value.

What this means when you buy a TV

First decide how big a screen you really need, because size is the biggest driver of running cost. Then compare star ratings only between TVs of the same size, since that is where a higher rating translates directly into a lower bill. For 51 to 55 inch TVs, models with 3.5 stars or fewer use a median of 513 kWh a year against 255 kWh for 6 stars or more, about $74 a year. Weigh the purchase price against the running cost over the years you will keep the TV.

Energy figures come from supplier-registered test results and use a standard 10 hours a day. Cost figures use 28.55 cents per kWh and your own use and tariff will differ.
